The local numbers behind your business, read from the original record.

What homes sold for in each town. What the salon down the road charges. What an HVAC permit costs in Ross, and whether West View asks for one at all. Primary sources. Not news search.

Every figure carries its source, its place and its date. If it can't be cited, it doesn't run.

01

County deeds and house cards

Every recorded sale in the six towns, joined to the county's own record of the house: year built, heating, central air. A deed the county has already marked as a family transfer stays out.

02

Township fee schedules

Each town's adopted permit fees, cited by name and date. Where a town doesn't require the permit, we say so, and we say who told us.

03

State licenses

Salon licenses from the State Board of Cosmetology and liquor license filings from the PLCB, both checked every Friday against a running registry.

04

Shop menus

What salons actually post on their own price lists, read shop by shop, with the read date on every price.

05

Minutes, meeting packets and check registers

Read for what was filed and what was decided. An agenda tells us what is scheduled, and we print it as scheduled. Only the minutes tell us what was decided. The check registers inside the packets show where each town's money went.

06

Sheriff and estate filings

The county sheriff sale list and Register of Wills filings, reported by property address. We don't print people's names.

And whatever else the record turns up. When a packet we already read holds something that matters to our readers, such as a resolution, a contract or a filing, it runs, with the document named and dated.

Who's filing this

Written by someone who lives here.

Fred Mason

West View, PA · Founder & sole reporter

I live in West View and spend my working life in financial-services operations, the part of the job that's about reading records closely and making sure the numbers hold up. The North Hills Brief is that same discipline pointed at my own neighborhood.

Small businesses in the North Hills are easy to overlook. There's no dedicated local desk covering what happens in six different council chambers every month. I built this to close that gap, starting with the houses that change hands and the shops on the main streets, with more trades to follow.

It's a small operation by design: one person reading the primary source, every week, so you don't have to.
